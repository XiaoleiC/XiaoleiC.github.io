Xiaolei (Alex) Chu

Hi — I'm Xiaolei (Alex)! I'm a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate at UC Berkeley, in the Department of Civil & Environmental Engineering, fortunate to work with Prof. Ziqi Wang.

Prior to my Ph.D., I received rigorous training in mechanics through my B.S. (with honors) at Southwest Jiaotong University and M.S. (with honors) at Tongji University, where I built a solid foundation in structural dynamics and probabilistic methods. During the first three years of my Ph.D., I focused on statistical physics, studying phase transitions and collective behavior in complex systems via mean-field theory and the renormalization group. Through this work, I discovered deep connections between statistical physics and modern AI — from energy-based models and diffusion processes to sampling and variational inference — which naturally led me to pivot toward AI research.

My current interests center on generative models (diffusion / flow), reinforcement learning, and LLM post-training. I'm always open to research collaborations, especially on 3D vision, generative models, and various RL stuff. Please feel free to reach out!

Research Interests

My research sits at the intersection of generative models, statistical physics, and uncertainty quantification, with applications to natural-hazard modeling and human–AI collective behavior. Current focus areas:

  • Large Language Models. Multimodal sentiment analysis; post-training (SFT, RLHF, RLVR).
  • Generative Models. Foundation models for natural-hazard generation (ground motion, hurricanes); text-to-image generation.
  • Statistical Physics. Emotional phase transitions; mean-field theory; renormalization group.
  • Uncertainty Quantification. Sequential Monte Carlo; Hamiltonian Monte Carlo.
